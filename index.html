Permit Notebook Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 23, 2026
Web address of this page: https://felixasencioencephalon.github.io/permit-notebook-florida-privacy/

This is the privacy policy for the Android app Permit Notebook (package name com.permitnotebook.florida). It tells you what the app keeps, what it sends, and what choices you have. It is written to be read by teens and parents.

The short version

  • You do not need an account. We never ask for your name, email, phone number or address in the app.
  • Your study progress, driving log and settings stay on your phone. We do not run a server and we cannot see them.
  • The app shows small banner ads through Google AdMob. When an ad loads, Google's software in the app sends some data to Google. Section 3 lists it.
  • We do not sell your data. We do not use any analytics or crash-reporting tools of our own.
  • Permit Notebook is not from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), the State of Florida or any government agency.

1. Who we are

Permit Notebook is made by an individual developer, Felix Asencio, who publishes on Google Play as Encephalon-Dev.

Contact for privacy questions: appdev@encephalon.net

2. What the app keeps on your phone

The app saves these things in its own storage on your phone (a local database and a settings file). They are not sent to us.

  • Your answers in practice tests, mock exams, review lists, bookmarks and flashcards, and your paused sessions.
  • Your mock exam history and scores.
  • Your driving log: the date, length and day or night of each drive you add.
  • Your ticks on the test-day checklist.
  • Your settings: theme, text and motion options, reading speed, vibration, study reminder and exam options.
  • The question-of-the-day record, so the app knows if you answered today.

Study reminders are local notifications. Your phone makes them at the time you pick. No server sends them. The app asks for notification permission only when you turn the reminder on.

The home-screen widget shows the question of the day. It reads from the same data on your phone.

Android phones can back up app data to your Google account and copy it to a new phone. Your progress and settings in the app can be part of that backup, under your phone's own backup settings. That backup is handled by Google and Android, not by us. You can turn it off in your phone's settings.

3. Ads, and the data Google collects

Permit Notebook is free. It pays for itself with banner ads from Google AdMob. Ads appear on the Home screen, the Stats screen and the results screens. They do not appear on question or explanation screens. There are no full-screen ads.

When the app asks for an ad, Google's AdMob software inside the app collects and uses data, and Google receives it. According to Google's published guidance for the AdMob software, this includes:

  • IP address (which can give a rough idea of your general location).
  • How you use the app and the ads, such as app launches, taps and ad views.
  • Diagnostic information, such as app launch time and crashes or hangs.
  • Device and other identifiers, such as an app set ID.

Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google says this data is encrypted in transit.

What we have set up:

  • Ad requests are marked for a teen audience (Google's "TEEN" setting), and ads are limited to a maximum content rating of PG.
  • Because ads are marked for teens, Google turns off personalized ads and remarketing for them. The ads you see are based on the app, not on you.
  • The app does not use your Android advertising ID. We removed that permission. Android tells apps without it to use a blank ID.
  • The Google ads software still adds two Android permissions for ad measurement, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS. These belong to Android's Privacy Sandbox. They are not the advertising ID.
  • The app sets ad sound to muted.

How Google uses information from apps that use its services is explained here: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites (page title: "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services"). Google's own privacy policy is at https://policies.google.com/privacy.

After you pass a mock exam, the app may ask Google Play to show its "rate this app" card. Google decides whether to show it and handles your rating and any review. The app does not see what you choose or write.

4. Your ad choices (consent)

The app uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to ask for your choice where the law requires it.

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google shows a consent form. You can say yes, no, or manage options. The app works the same whichever you pick.
  • In some US states, Google offers a privacy options form about the sale or sharing of your data ("My data preferences").
  • Where no form is needed, the app does not show one.

If the form cannot load (for example, you are offline), ads do not show until you have made a choice.

You can change your choice at any time: open Settings in the app, then Privacy and ads, then Ad privacy choices. That row is turned off in places where no form is needed.

You can also reset your Android advertising settings in your phone's Settings under Google, then Ads.

5. Sharing your driving log

The app only shares your driving log when you tap Share. You see the exact text first. You can add a spreadsheet file (CSV). The app makes the file in a temporary folder on your phone and hands it to the app you pick from Android's share sheet. We do not receive it. After you share it, that other app's own privacy policy applies. The app clears its temporary share files the next time you share and when it starts.

6. What we do not do

  • We do not collect your name, email, phone number, address, contacts, photos, microphone, camera or precise location.
  • We do not run an account system or a server that stores your data.
  • We do not use analytics or crash-reporting tools of our own. Google Play may give us crash and slow-response numbers from its own Android vitals system. These are summaries and are not linked to you by us.
  • We do not sell your personal data.
  • We do not use your data to build a profile of you.

If you email us (section 10), we see your email address and what you write. We use it only to answer you.

7. Keeping and deleting your data

Data in the app stays on your phone until you delete it.

To erase it: open Settings, then Your data, then Delete my progress. This erases your answers, review lists, bookmarks, exam history, paused sessions, driving log and checklist ticks from your phone. Your settings stay. You can also clear the app's storage in Android's app settings, or uninstall the app.

A copy may remain in your phone's Google backup until your phone's next backup. If the app is restored from an old backup, old progress can come back. You can manage or delete backups in your Google account and phone settings.

We hold no copy of your app data, so we cannot delete it for you. To manage data that Google collects through ads, use Google's tools at https://myaccount.google.com and the choices in section 4.

8. Children

Permit Notebook is for people studying for the Florida learner permit, mainly teens and their parents or guardians. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. A parent or guardian who thinks a child under 13 has used the app can contact us (section 10).

9. Security

Your data stays on your phone and is protected by your phone's own security, such as its screen lock. Data sent by the Google ads software is encrypted in transit, according to Google. No system is perfectly secure.
